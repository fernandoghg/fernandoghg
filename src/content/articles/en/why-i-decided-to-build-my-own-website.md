Over the years, I've had an online presence in different forms, but I'd never quite built a space of my own where I could bring together my interests, projects and experiences.

As a computer engineer, the idea of having a personal website had always appealed to me. Not so much as a professional showcase, but as a place to share what I do, what interests me and what I learn along the way.

Over time, I'd also accumulated projects and ideas that deserved more than being left in a folder on my computer.

That's how I came to create **fernandoghg.com**.

## A Space of My Own, Without Unnecessary Complications

I didn't want to build a particularly sophisticated website or turn its maintenance into another job.

I was looking for something simple: a fast website that was easy to navigate, worked well on computers and mobile devices, and could grow gradually.

I also wanted editorial freedom: the chance to write about technology, software development, video games, simulation or anything else that interested me, without having to fit everything into a single subject.

But I didn't want to show only the finished results of my projects. I also want to explain how I develop them, the decisions I make, the difficulties I encounter and the things that don't go as planned. I believe those experiences could be useful to others trying to do something similar.

The idea wasn't to publish constantly, but to write whenever I had something worth sharing.

Above all, I wanted to avoid making the website more complicated to maintain than its content.

## More Than a Personal Introduction

As the project progressed, I became increasingly certain that the website shouldn't be limited to a homepage with a few personal details.

I also wanted to use it to present and document other projects: not just their results, but the development process, the decisions made, the difficulties encountered and what I learned along the way.

One of them is **[Aldarte](/en/projects/aldarte/)**, an experiment in voluntary participation that lets people express how they feel through one anonymous vote a day and view the aggregated results from participants.

Another is **[The Last Lighthouse Keeper: Worlds of Infinity](/en/projects/the-last-lighthouse-keeper/)**, a fantasy adventure I'm developing with Godot, where I'm also exploring the possibilities of using artificial intelligence to create video games.

Over time, I'll add other projects and ideas that I find interesting, without requiring them all to be related or to fit a single theme.

I intend to share their progress openly, including successes, mistakes and changes of direction along the way.

## A Project That Will Keep Growing

fernandoghg.com already has a working technical foundation, but that doesn't mean it's finished.

A personal website is, to a large extent, an ongoing project. Its content will evolve as new ideas, experiences and projects emerge that are worth sharing.

Building this website has also given me the chance to experiment with a rapidly changing approach to software development: using artificial intelligence as a tool and delegating much of the technical work to it.

That experience deserves an article of its own, where I'll explain how I've worked with ChatGPT and Codex, which decisions I've delegated and what I've learned during the process.

For now, this first article explains where it all began.

I didn't simply want to have a website.

**I wanted to build a space of my own and use the process as an opportunity to keep learning.**
